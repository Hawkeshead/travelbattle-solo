WHAT THIS IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT

This is a simulator-derived profile over seeds 1 to 3, not the Section 4 table
from the seed 551846676 match export. It cannot be that, for two reasons worth
stating rather than quietly working around.

The seed fixes the dice, not the map. Terrain comes from a separate source, so
re-running 551846676 plays a different battle on different ground. A single-seed
term table is not reproducible and would be a baseline that cannot be
re-derived, which is no baseline at all.

To preserve the actual numbers from the 551846676 export, paste the Section 4
list in and it gets appended below as a separate, clearly labelled block.

TWO THINGS THAT BEAR ON READING ANY FUTURE COMPARISON

Long matches are over-represented twice over. The report polls the debug log on
a real-time interval while the game's timers are collapsed, so a fast match has
turns overwritten before they are sampled: 401 turns gave 349 moves, 47 turns
gave 21. Long matches also simply last longer. Since long matches are usually
STALLED matches, these totals lean toward the terms that dominate a stall
(brigadierTrail, freeStrandedUnit) and understate the terms that decide a
fighting match. A like-for-like comparison against this baseline is still valid,
because the same bias applies to both sides of it.

Two of three sample matches stalled at the 400-turn cap. That is the known
absorbing-state problem (a unit off the chain cannot move, and moving is the
only way back), and it is the dominant fact about this build's simulator
behaviour, whatever the term ranks say.

MISSIONPULL IS THE MOST DECISIVE TERM IN THE ARMY

At 29.8% of sampled moves overall and 49.0% in the manoeuvring match, missionPull
separates more moves than anything else. This matters for what comes next: the
army-level plan sets missions and missionPull targets and nothing below that, so
it acts directly on the single largest lever in the scoring. That cuts both ways.
It is why an army plan can be expected to change behaviour at all, and it is why
a wrong target choice will be expensive rather than merely inert.
